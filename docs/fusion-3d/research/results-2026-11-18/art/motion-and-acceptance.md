# Motion, camera and frame-pacing production sheet

All durations, distances and review thresholds in the Fusion direction below are proposed tuning values, except for the explicitly sourced sprite-row facts, glTF units, existing programme budgets and measured fixture results. A source sprite index is an asset offset. It cannot establish elapsed time by itself.

## Source-to-clip decisions

The pinned source tables cover 46 infantry declarations, 33 motion-table families and 953 family/action entries across Tiberian Dawn and Red Alert. Several units point to the same family. For each imported row key the record by release, INI ID, animation family, action, availability and source line. Preserve unused/one-frame placeholder entries and the semantic standing/prone release indices. Never generate a new unit ability simply because a constructor declares an index. Red Alert’s code rejects a zero-length action and offsets directional frames by stage and facing stride. Standing and prone combat can use different launch markers. ## Authoring and event contract

1. Bind a stable entity ID and revision to an ordinary Y-up right-handed glTF in metres. Put the semantic forward and authoring root in the manifest. Export in-place locomotion, one fixed gameplay anchor, bone or rigid-node hierarchy, world-space diagnostic bounds and named emitter/selection/service sockets. For skeletal rigs, test bind pose, normalized weights and animation across all mesh levels. For rigid machines, use separately pivoted wheels, turret, doors, brace and tools. [glTF specification](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html)
2. Give each clip an ID, family, allowed state, duration, loop flag, entry/exit pose, available track masks, priority and synchronization rule. Record semantic `plant_L`, `plant_R`, `release`, `door_clear`, `buckle`, `service_contact` markers only when the clip truly has those events. Inject the authoritative event ID and tick into the presentation queue. Pose anticipation can start on an accepted state transition. Independent impact and damage events own their effects.
3. Interpolate between authoritative transforms from adjacent snapshots at the display cadence. Use normalised shortest rotation for angles/quaternions. Do not extrapolate across a surface change, discontinuity, death or teleport. On corrected snapshots blend small visual offsets only, for a finite window. Settle exactly on the authority pose immediately for a death, denied crossing or deck collapse. Keep footprint, selection and queue state stable during the correction.
4. Treat idle, travel, turning and body aim as ordinary loops. Transition to higher-priority death or structural collapse at the next presented frame after receipt. An uncommitted action may return to idle. A committed action that gets interrupted retains its authoritative effects. Key temporary effects by (entity ID, event ID, effect slot). On replay/reconnect initialise from the displayed snapshot, restore persistent loops and clear transient emitters whose life ended. Prevent duplicate discharge, egress, repair completion and smoke.
5. For close/far or high/low quality, keep clip clock and normalized phase. Change palette/skeleton/mesh sampling while preserving pivot, silhouette, ground contact, team mark, target socket and impact time. Keep a low-complexity 3D mesh at ordinary tactical distance. Use an original sprite for particles, decals, icons and optional extreme-distance impostors only when camera angle and silhouette pass. Flight, bridge deck/underpass picking and 360° rotation need real 3D at supported gameplay zoom.
6. Measure at a known pixel height, independent of asset export resolution. Candidate review sample is infantry at 12/20/36 px and normal vehicles at 22/40/72 px tall on physical screenshots. Match the programme’s approved camera on a fixed map. Record viewport CSS pixels, device pixel ratio, render scale, elevation, yaw and zoom. A selection halo, overlaid name or bloom can conceal an unrecognisable rig. Repeat silhouette work without them.

## Animation family sheet

| Family | Pose sequence and source of motion | Shot and objective check |
|---|---|---|
| Humanoid walk / run | Author cyclic asymmetric movement. Walk phases at 0% left contact, 12.5% loading, 25% passing, 37.5% rise, 50% right contact, mirrored second half, 100% closed loop. Calibrate strides in metres per cycle from measured rig contacts. Use authored running flight phase when appropriate. Blend rate by speed / stride, bounded by a walk–run transition. | Render lateral/diagonal motion on level ground and ramp. During a planted contact, horizontal drift relative to ground ≤3% of leg length for this test. No root drift after ten cycles. Compare stop on left vs right foot and a reversal. |
| Crawl, prone, crouch, leave cover | Lower the centre without sinking the limbs. Use alternate hand/elbow contacts and ground-following visual offsets constrained by the current authoritative surface. | In a dark field verify prone actor remains distinguishable from a wreck. A command in middle of the transition must use the sim’s accepted posture at that tick. |
| Wheeled / tracked | Rotate wheels by distance/radius (radians); accumulate signed visual travel. Track links/UVs move opposite ground-relative motion at the tread contact. Differential turning can move the two sides in opposite directions. Chassis pitch/roll is a small visual response to contacts. | 4 m travel, stop, reverse, then pivot. Compare expected and observed wheel rotation using actual mesh radius. Body must not float between coplanar terrain chunks. |
| Walker | Create separate left and right stride and support phases, lower mass over the stance limb and amortise pelvis lift. Lock feet in planted windows; cap stepping near a ledge. Multi-legged units need named support groups and a static stable idle. | Side and ¾ view over stairs and ramp. Feet should settle before next support lifts where the authored gait requires it; reject visibly floating pelvis at low update level. |
| Turret / gimbal / projectile cue | Independent base yaw, local aim pivot, elevation and brief recoil with hard mechanical stops. Lead anticipation responds to ordered state; show discharge and recoil on the confirmed release. A 350°→10° yaw takes the short 20° arc. | Turn chassis during aim. Ensure emitter is at actual muzzle, no silhouette reversal, no duplicate effect on prediction correction. Trace ticks for release/impact. |
| Rotorcraft / fixed wing / transport | Rotor hub motion/persistence depends on visibility and sample rate. Replace high-frequency blades with a low-opacity disc at distance. Aircraft banking shows turn intent; level on final approach. Align landing gear, door clearance and rotor/body extents. | Hover, climb over deck, bank, depart and land. During transport egress, ramp/door clearance is visible before any unit exits. Selecting a ground target must ignore an aircraft shadow. |
| Ship / submarine / amphibious change | Couple propulsion and wake strength to actual travel and state. Use gentle vertical motion around authoritative water height. Centre wake on stern, remove it on hide/submerge as rules require. Keep shore/water graphics consistent through form change. | Cross shore, pass under intact deck and surface. Reject an enemy wake visible in unexplored space, dry-land spray, or ship touching deck underside. |
| Converter / deployer / epic unit | Choose explicit fold/unfold pivots and end-state bounds. Move braces before weight-bearing parts, then expose the service socket or operational posture. Provide getting-up and interrupted mid-transition states. | Compare start/end silhouette labels in grayscale. Kill or cancel in three phases and confirm permitted domain/footprint are determined once by authority. |
| Factory / infantry production | Show a ready rhythm then physical door opening, sufficient clearance, egress and closure. A blocked exit holds the waiting state. New actor and foundation remain independent. | Place a vehicle on the exit, clear it, restore a save at the opening pose and run a second queue item. No duplicated visual or produced unit. |
| Resource / refinery / power | Animate pump/rotor or processing stage at low frequency. A load/dock/unload event changes one readability cue independently of team tint. Use a strong power-down pose on the moving mass. | Compare no-power, depleted field, full cargo, returning and interrupted docking. Fog boundary suppresses hidden service/queue information. |
| Structural destruction / reconstruction | Design differing roof and wall silhouettes for damage; staged dust, major collapse and stable landmark rubble. Set placement origin and permitted surface footprint per state. Repair can show scaffold even when deck is closed. | Trigger destruction in a crowd and across a fog edge. Test reduced motion/no particles and inspect route. Reject an invisible obstacle mismatch or a transient effect acting as traversable geometry. |
| Team paint, tint, stealth and accessibility | Retain a sufficiently large team panel across active poses. Combine ownership colour with shape/pattern under allied vs enemy inspection. Stealth and reveal feedback are products of visibility admission, with stable persistence. | Repeat identifiers in grayscale, common colour-vision simulation and lowest effects quality. Inspect reduced flash/camera shake and visible feedback with sound muted. |

**Original reference sequence.** The service tractor deploys in 1.70 s. Both braces reach within 11.4 mm of the ground by 0.60 s before the boom rises. The tool tip height changes from 1.36 to 2.51689 m at full extension. The 2.00 s scan is an independent loop and 1.70 s retract returns to its original coordinates. This is an engineering motion study. It contains no unit logic or historical timing claim. The 5.5 s review clip was sampled at 12 fps; do not judge 30/60 Hz frame pacing from that export. ## Frame, performance and acceptance capture

Use the project's target battle of 256 mixed units and 64 buildings. Fix seed, time, visible faction mix, bridge section and issue commands from the same replay. Store the reference map, rules profile, asset manifest hash, settings and source revision next to the run. Primary scene should include infantry under canopy, long hulls on deck, ship below, moving aircraft shadows, a new production exit, harvest cycle, smoke and several faction materials together. Warm up offline shader/resource loading, repeat first-load separately and record time to first response. Record ten minutes of active scene after warm-up and the independent twenty-minute thermal run. Desktop targets p95 ≤16.7 ms and p99 ≤33.3 ms. Mobile targets p95 ≤33.3 ms and p99 ≤50 ms. Use existing CPU p95 limits 8/12 ms (desktop/mobile) and texture/geometry/render-target budgets 512/192 MiB. Report any extra budget allocated for shadows, aliasing and thermal stability against the existing budgets.

Suppress nonessential camera jolts, sudden full-screen flash or particle sheets; preserve command, recovery and readiness cues. | Same command script and visibility results in every setting; record flashes and overlapping high-contrast FX for manual review. |

For frame pacing, log successive visible requestAnimationFrame timestamps and the CPU slice measured independently with a monotonic clock. Report nearest-rank p50/p95/p99, worst frame, count over 50/100 ms, longest consecutive slow run, and worst one-minute window. Retain the full trace and note background/hidden intervals, orientation switches and missing data. Score foreground runs continuously; report interruptions separately. Use GPU timer queries only where available, discard disjoint samples and state the instrument’s coverage. Do not subtract two unrelated clocks. The simulation tick period cannot represent render frame duration. A sampled frame interval combines scheduling, presentation and rendering. Report hardware GPU timing separately from estimated GPU bytes, transfer size and real frame pacing.

For input, record pointer event to local highlight (existing p95 ≤100 ms desktop, ≤150 ms mobile), server admission, first accepted movement tick and visible movement as separate timestamps. Render-hint availability depends on platform. For latency-critical review film one physical interaction beside the screen at known frame rate to verify the visible response measured by instrumentation. Three.js shader compile/relink, uploads and garbage collection can cause spikes with a low median. Repeat cold-cache arrival of each faction family and record the first newly exposed actor after fog of war. No device or browser pass can be awarded from modelling-tool frame rate or a preview exported at constant cadence.

## Specialist execution order

| Owner | Bounded task and definition of done |
|---|---|
| Historian | Pick one release/entity/variant. Supply the source event row, locators, clearly labelled unknowns and tactical reference capture. Review ability-vs-animation separately. |
| Concept / look development | Deliver eight silhouettes, three camera sizes and regional palette/lighting swatches. Art director signs a named silhouette choice and records original design rationale. |
| Modeller / rigger | Deliver metric source file, glTF, per-LOD budget, pivot/socket registry and weighted/rigid hierarchy. Inspect all axis signs in the importer and provide no drift on the root. |
| Animator | Implement just the assigned family and interruption table. Supply contact curves, real-time clip data, source-to-Fusion mapping and before/on/after commitment recording. |
| Renderer engineer | Bind one-way source state, fog filtering, interpolation and quality switching in the actual playable entry point. Log presentation evidence and prove deterministic state hashes match across visual tiers. |
| QA / oversight | Independently load the model and run captures A–G applicable to this family. Return exact fixture, device, metric, observed failure and replay tick. The director signs off only for the named version, release profile and targets. |
